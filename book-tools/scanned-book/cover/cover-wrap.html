Keeping the HeartFLAVEL
The greatest difficulty after conversion is to keep the heart with God.

Flavel wrote this little book in 1667 for his flock at Dartmouth, when the law had shut him out of their pulpit. It shows what it is to keep the heart, why it is the great business of a Christian’s life, and how to keep it in twelve seasons that try every believer: prosperity and adversity, public danger and private want, injuries and provocations, temptation, spiritual darkness, suffering for Christ, and sickness and death.

This edition follows the American Tract Society text, with footnotes and a glossary for old words.

John Flavel (1627–1691) was a Puritan minister at Dartmouth, Devon. Put out of his pulpit in 1662, he kept preaching to his people in private through years of persecution, and wrote for them what he could not always say in person.
