Scripture is quoted from the Berean Standard Bible, kept closer to the author’s own Bible where his argument rests on its wording; capitals for God in quotations (He, His) follow the BSB. “You+” marks every plural “you” (the older “ye”); a plain “you” is singular or general.

Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God

This verse threatens God’s vengeance on the wicked, unbelieving Israelites. They were God’s visible people and lived under the means of grace. Yet in spite of all God’s wonderful works toward them, they remained, as verse 28 says, “devoid of counsel,” with “no understanding among them.” Though heaven cultivated them in every way, they brought forth bitter and poisonous fruit, as the two verses just before the text show. The words I have chosen for my text, “Their foot shall slide in due time,” seem to imply the following things about the punishment and destruction these wicked Israelites were exposed to.

  1. That they were always exposed to destruction, as one who stands or walks in slippery places is always exposed to falling. This is implied in the way their destruction is pictured as coming on them: by their foot sliding. The same thing is expressed in Ps 73:18: “Surely You set them on slick ground; You cast them down into ruin.”
  2. It implies that they were always exposed to sudden, unexpected destruction. A man who walks in slippery places is liable to fall at any moment. He cannot foresee from one moment to the next whether he will stand or fall, and when he does fall, he falls all at once, without warning. This too is expressed in Psalm 73:18–19: “Surely You set them on slick ground; You cast them down into ruin. How suddenly they are laid waste!”
  3. Another thing implied is that they are liable to fall of themselves, without being thrown down by the hand of another, just as a man who stands or walks on slippery ground needs nothing but his own weight to throw him down.
  4. That the reason they have not already fallen, and do not fall now, is only that God’s appointed time has not come. For it is said that when that due time, or appointed time, comes, their foot shall slide. Then they will be left to fall, as their own weight inclines them. God will not hold them up in these slippery places any longer but will let them go. And then, at that very instant, they will fall into destruction, like a man standing on such slippery, sloping ground at the edge of a pit: he cannot stand alone, and when he is let go, he falls at once and is lost.

The observation from these words that I would now press is this: “There is nothing that keeps wicked men out of hell at any one moment but the sheer good pleasure of God.” By the sheer good pleasure of God I mean his sovereign pleasure, his unconstrained will, restrained by no obligation and hindered by no difficulty of any kind, no more than if nothing but God’s will alone had any hand at all, in the least degree or in any respect whatever, in preserving wicked men for one moment.

The truth of this observation may be seen from the following considerations.

  1. There is no lack of power in God to cast wicked men into hell at any moment. Men’s hands cannot be strong when God rises up. The strongest have no power to resist him, and no one can deliver out of his hands. He is not only able to cast wicked men into hell, but he can do it most easily. Sometimes an earthly prince has a great deal of difficulty subduing a rebel who has found a way to fortify himself and has made himself strong by the number of his followers. But it is not so with God. There is no fortress that is any defense against the power of God. Though hand join in hand, and vast multitudes of God’s enemies combine and band together, they are easily broken in pieces. They are like great heaps of light chaff before the whirlwind, or large quantities of dry stubble before devouring flames. We find it easy to tread on and crush a worm that we see crawling on the earth. It is just as easy for us to cut or singe a slender thread that something hangs by. That is how easy it is for God, when he pleases, to cast his enemies down to hell. What are we, that we should think to stand before him at whose rebuke the earth trembles, and before whom the rocks are thrown down?

  2. They deserve to be cast into hell, so divine justice never stands in the way; it makes no objection to God’s using his power at any moment to destroy them. Indeed, on the contrary, justice calls aloud for an infinite punishment of their sins. Divine justice says of the tree that brings forth such grapes of Sodom, “Therefore cut it down! Why should it use up the soil?” (Luke 13:7). The sword of divine justice is brandished over their heads every moment, and nothing but the hand of sovereign mercy, and God’s will alone, holds it back.

  3. They are already under a sentence of condemnation to hell. They not only justly deserve to be cast down there, but the sentence of the law of God, that eternal and unchangeable rule of righteousness that God has fixed between himself and mankind, has gone out against them and stands against them. So they are already bound over to hell: “Whoever does not believe has already been condemned” (John 3:18). So every unconverted man properly belongs to hell. That is his place; he is from there: “You are from below” (John 8:23). And he is bound there; it is the place that justice, and God’s word, and the sentence of his unchangeable law assign to him.

  4. They are now the objects of that very same anger and wrath of God that is expressed in the torments of hell. The reason they do not go down to hell at each moment is not that God, in whose power they are, is not then very angry with them, as angry as he is with many miserable creatures now tormented in hell, who feel and bear the fierceness of his wrath there. Indeed, God is a great deal more angry with great numbers who are now on earth—yes, doubtless with many who are now in this congregation, who may perhaps be at ease—than he is with many of those who are now in the flames of hell.

    So it is not because God is unmindful of their wickedness and is not angered by it that he does not let loose his hand and cut them off. God is not just like them, though they may imagine he is. The wrath of God burns against them; their damnation does not slumber. The pit is prepared, the fire is made ready, the furnace is now hot, ready to receive them; the flames now rage and glow. The glittering sword is sharpened and held over them, and the pit has opened its mouth under them.

  5. The devil stands ready to fall on them and seize them as his own at whatever moment God permits him. They belong to him; he has their souls in his possession and under his dominion. Scripture represents them as his goods (Luke 11:21). The devils watch them; they are always beside them, at their right hand. They stand waiting for them like greedy, hungry lions that see their prey and expect to have it, but are kept back for the present. If God were to withdraw his hand, by which they are restrained, they would fly at their poor souls in one moment. The old serpent is gaping for them; hell opens its mouth wide to receive them; and if God permitted it, they would be swiftly swallowed up and lost.

  6. In the souls of wicked men there are hellish principles reigning that would immediately kindle and flame out into hell fire, if it were not for God’s restraints. A foundation for the torments of hell is laid in the very nature of carnal men. Those corrupt principles reign in power in them and are in full possession of them, and they are seeds of hell fire. These principles are active and powerful, exceedingly violent in their nature. If it were not for God’s restraining hand on them, they would soon break out. They would flame out in the same way as the same corruptions, the same enmity, do in the hearts of damned souls, and would produce the same torments in them as they do in the damned. In Scripture the souls of the wicked are compared to the troubled sea (Isa 57:20). For the present, God restrains their wickedness by his mighty power, as he does the raging waves of the troubled sea, saying, “You may come this far, but no farther.” But if God were to withdraw that restraining power, it would soon carry all before it. Sin is the ruin and misery of the soul; it is destructive in its nature. If God left it without restraint, nothing else would be needed to make the soul perfectly miserable. The corruption of man’s heart is immoderate and boundless in its fury. While wicked men live here, it is like fire pent up by God’s restraints; if it were let loose, it would set on fire the course of nature. And as the heart is now a cesspool of sin, so if sin were not restrained, it would immediately turn the soul into a fiery oven, or a furnace of fire and brimstone.

  7. It is no security to wicked men for one moment that there are no visible means of death at hand. It is no security to a natural man that he is now in health, that he does not see any way he could now immediately leave the world by some accident, and that there is no visible danger of any kind in his circumstances. The manifold and continual experience of the world in all ages shows that this is no evidence that a man is not on the very brink of eternity, or that his next step will not be into another world. The unseen, unthought-of ways and means by which people go suddenly out of the world are innumerable and inconceivable. Unconverted men walk over the pit of hell on a rotten covering, and there are innumerable places in this covering so weak that they will not bear their weight, and these places are not seen. The arrows of death fly unseen at noonday; the sharpest sight cannot discern them. God has so many different, unsearchable ways of taking wicked men out of the world and sending them to hell that there is nothing to suggest that God would need to go to the expense of a miracle, or go out of the ordinary course of his providence, to destroy any wicked man at any moment. All the means by which sinners go out of the world are so in God’s hands, and so universally and absolutely subject to his power and determination, that whether sinners go to hell at any moment depends not one bit less on the will of God alone than if means were never used or had nothing at all to do with the case.

  8. Natural men’s prudence and care to preserve their own lives, or the care of others to preserve them, do not secure them for a moment. Divine providence and universal experience also bear witness to this. Here is clear evidence that men’s own wisdom is no security to them against death: if it were otherwise, we would see some difference between the wise and shrewd men of the world and others in how liable they are to early and unexpected death. But how is it in fact? “Alas, the wise man will die just like the fool!” (Eccl 2:16).

  9. All the pains and schemes wicked men use to escape hell, while they continue to reject Christ and so remain wicked men, do not secure them from hell for one moment. Almost every natural man who hears of hell flatters himself that he will escape it. He depends on himself for his own security. He flatters himself in what he has done, in what he is doing now, or in what he intends to do. Each one lays out plans in his own mind for how he will avoid damnation, and flatters himself that he is planning well for himself and that his schemes will not fail. They do hear that only a few are saved, and that the greater part of the men who have died before are gone to hell. But each one imagines that he lays out his plans for his own escape better than others have done. He does not intend to come to that place of torment. He says within himself that he intends to take effectual care, and to order matters for himself so that he will not fail.

    But the foolish children of men miserably deceive themselves in their own schemes and in their confidence in their own strength and wisdom; they trust in nothing but a shadow. The greater part of those who have lived before under the same means of grace, and are now dead, are undoubtedly gone to hell. And it was not because they were not as wise as those who are now alive; it was not because they did not lay out their plans as well to secure their own escape. If we could speak with them and ask them, one by one, whether they expected, when they were alive and used to hear about hell, ever to be the subjects of that misery, we would doubtless hear one after another reply: “No, I never intended to come here. I had laid out matters otherwise in my mind. I thought I would plan well for myself; I thought my scheme was good. I intended to take effectual care, but it came on me unexpectedly. I did not expect it at that time or in that way; it came like a thief. Death outwitted me; God’s wrath was too quick for me. Oh, my cursed foolishness! I was flattering myself and pleasing myself with vain dreams of what I would do later; and when I was saying, ‘Peace and safety,’ then sudden destruction came on me.”

  10. God has laid himself under no obligation, by any promise, to keep any natural man out of hell for one moment. God has certainly made no promises, either of eternal life or of any deliverance or preservation from eternal death, except those contained in the covenant of grace, the promises given in Christ, in whom all the promises are yes and amen. But surely those have no share in the promises of the covenant of grace who are not the children of the covenant, who do not believe in any of the promises, and have no share in the Mediator of the covenant.

So, whatever some have imagined and claimed about promises made to natural men’s earnest seeking and knocking, it is plain and clear that whatever pains a natural man takes in religion, and whatever prayers he makes, until he believes in Christ, God is under no obligation of any kind to keep him from eternal destruction for a moment. So this is how natural men are held in the hand of God over the pit of hell. They have deserved the fiery pit and are already sentenced to it. God is dreadfully provoked; his anger is as great toward them as toward those who are actually suffering the execution of the fierceness of his wrath in hell. They have done nothing in the least to appease or lessen that anger, and God is not in the least bound by any promise to hold them up for one moment. The devil is waiting for them, hell is gaping for them, the flames gather and flash about them and would gladly lay hold of them and swallow them up. The fire pent up in their own hearts is struggling to break out. They have no share in any Mediator; there are no means within reach that can be any security to them. In short, they have no refuge, nothing to take hold of. All that preserves them every moment is the sheer, unconstrained will, and the uncovenanted, unobliged forbearance, of an incensed God.

Application

This fearful subject may serve to awaken the unconverted in this congregation. What you+ have heard is the case of every one of you+ who is outside of Christ. That world of misery, that lake of burning brimstone, is spread out beneath you+. There is the dreadful pit, glowing with the flames of God’s wrath. There is hell’s wide mouth, gaping open. You+ have nothing to stand on and nothing to hold on to. There is nothing between you+ and hell but the air. Only the power and the sheer good pleasure of God hold you+ up.

You are probably not aware of this. You find that you are kept out of hell, but you do not see the hand of God in it. Instead you look at other things, such as the good state of your bodily constitution, your care of your own life, and the means you use to preserve yourself. But in reality these things are nothing. If God were to withdraw his hand, they would do no more to keep you from falling than the thin air does to hold up a person who is hanging in it.

Your wickedness makes you, as it were, heavy as lead, and makes you tend downward with great weight and pressure toward hell. If God were to let you go, you would immediately sink and swiftly descend and plunge into the bottomless gulf. Your healthy constitution, your own care and prudence, your best schemes, and all your righteousness would have no more power to hold you up and keep you out of hell than a spider’s web would have to stop a falling rock. Were it not for the sovereign pleasure of God, the earth would not bear you for one moment, for you are a burden to it. The creation groans with you. The creation is made subject to the bondage of your corruption, not willingly. The sun does not willingly shine on you to give you light to serve sin and Satan. The earth does not willingly yield her increase to satisfy your lusts, nor is it willingly a stage on which your wickedness is acted out. The air does not willingly serve you with breath to keep the flame of life burning in your vitals while you spend your life in the service of God’s enemies. God’s creatures are good, and were made for men to serve God with. They do not willingly serve any other purpose, and they groan when they are abused for purposes so directly contrary to their nature and end. And the world would spew you out, were it not for the sovereign hand of him who has subjected it in hope. Black clouds of God’s wrath are now hanging directly over your heads, full of the dreadful storm and heavy with thunder; and were it not for the restraining hand of God, the storm would immediately burst on you. For the present the sovereign pleasure of God holds back his rough wind; otherwise it would come with fury, your destruction would come like a whirlwind, and you would be like the chaff of the summer threshing floor.

The wrath of God is like great waters held back by a dam for the present. They grow more and more and rise higher and higher until an outlet is given. And the longer the stream is held back, the faster and more powerful its course once it is let loose. It is true that judgment against your evil deeds has not yet been carried out. The floods of God’s vengeance have been held back. But all the while your guilt is growing, and every day you are storing up more wrath. The waters keep rising and growing mightier. Nothing but the sheer good pleasure of God holds them back, though they are unwilling to be held back and press hard to go forward. If God were only to take his hand from the floodgate, it would fly open at once. The fiery floods of the fierceness and wrath of God would rush out with unimaginable fury and come upon you with almighty power. Even if your strength were ten thousand times greater than it is, even ten thousand times greater than the strength of the stoutest, sturdiest devil in hell, it would be nothing to withstand or endure it.

The bow of God’s wrath is bent, and the arrow made ready on the string. Justice aims the arrow at your+ heart and strains the bow, and nothing but the sheer good pleasure of God—and that of an angry God, without any promise or obligation at all—keeps the arrow one moment from being made drunk with your+ blood. So all of you+ who have never passed through a great change of heart by the mighty power of the Spirit of God on your+ souls, all of you+ who were never born again and made new creatures, and raised from being dead in sin to a state of new light and life, never known before, are in the hands of an angry God. However much you+ may have reformed your+ life in many things, and may have had religious affections, and may keep up a form of religion in your+ families, in your+ private devotions, and in the house of God, it is nothing but his sheer good pleasure that keeps you+ from being swallowed up in everlasting destruction this moment. However unconvinced you+ may now be of the truth of what you+ hear, before long you+ will be fully convinced of it. Those who were once in circumstances like yours+, and are now gone, see that it was so with them. For destruction came suddenly on most of them, when they expected nothing of it and while they were saying, “Peace and safety.” Now they see that the things they depended on for peace and safety were nothing but thin air and empty shadows.

The God who holds you over the pit of hell, much as one holds a spider or some loathsome insect over the fire, abhors you and is dreadfully provoked. His wrath toward you burns like fire. He looks on you as worthy of nothing but to be cast into the fire. His eyes are too pure to bear having you in his sight. You are ten thousand times more abominable in his eyes than the most hateful venomous serpent is in ours. You have offended him infinitely more than any stubborn rebel ever offended his prince. And yet nothing but his hand keeps you from falling into the fire every moment. There is no other reason that you did not go to hell last night, or that you were allowed to wake again in this world after you closed your eyes to sleep. There is no other reason you have not dropped into hell since you got up this morning, except that God’s hand has held you up. There is no other reason you have not gone to hell since you sat down here in the house of God, provoking his pure eyes by the sinful, wicked way you attend his solemn worship. Indeed, nothing else can be given as the reason why you do not drop down into hell this very moment.

O sinner! Consider the fearful danger you are in. It is a great furnace of wrath, a wide and bottomless pit full of the fire of wrath, that you are held over in the hand of that God whose wrath is provoked and incensed as much against you as against many of the damned in hell. You hang by a slender thread, with the flames of divine wrath flashing about it, ready every moment to singe it and burn it in two. You have no share in any Mediator, and nothing to lay hold of to save yourself, nothing to keep off the flames of wrath: nothing of your own, nothing you have ever done, nothing you can do, to persuade God to spare you for one moment. And consider here more particularly several things about that wrath you are in such danger of:

  1. Whose wrath it is. It is the wrath of the infinite God. If it were only the wrath of man, even of the most powerful prince, it would be comparatively little to be regarded. The wrath of kings is very much dreaded, especially of absolute monarchs, who have the possessions and lives of their subjects wholly in their power, to dispose of at their will alone. “The terror of a king is like the roar of a lion; whoever provokes him forfeits his own life” (Prov 20:2). The subject who greatly enrages an absolute prince is liable to suffer the most extreme torments that human skill can invent or human power can inflict. But the greatest earthly potentates, in their greatest majesty and strength, and when clothed in their greatest terrors, are only feeble, despicable worms of the dust in comparison with the great and almighty Creator and King of heaven and earth. They can do but little, even when most enraged and when they have exerted the utmost of their fury. All the kings of the earth are like grasshoppers before God; they are nothing, and less than nothing. Both their love and their hatred are to be despised. The wrath of the great King of kings is as much more terrible than theirs as his majesty is greater. “I tell you, My friends, do not be afraid of those who kill the body and after that can do no more. But I will show you whom you should fear: Fear the One who, after you have been killed, has authority to throw you into hell. Yes, I tell you, fear Him!” (Luke 12:4–5).

  2. It is the fierceness of his wrath that you are exposed to. We often read of the fury of God, as in Isaiah 59:18: “So He will repay according to their deeds: fury to His enemies.” So too in Isa 66:15: “For behold, the Lord will come with fire—His chariots are like a whirlwind—to execute His anger with fury and His rebuke with flames of fire.” And so in many other places. So in Rev 19:15 (KJV) we read of “the winepress of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God.” The words are exceedingly terrible. If the verse had only said “the wrath of God,” the words would have implied what is infinitely dreadful. But it is “the fierceness and wrath of God.” The fury of God! The fierceness of Jehovah! Oh, how dreadful that must be! Who can utter or conceive what such expressions carry in them! But it is also “the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God.” It is as though there would be a very great display of his almighty power in what the fierceness of his wrath would inflict, as though omnipotence would be, as it were, enraged and exerted, as men are accustomed to exert their strength in the fierceness of their wrath. Oh, then, what will the consequence be! What will become of the poor worms who will suffer it! Whose hands can be strong? And whose heart can endure? To what a dreadful, inexpressible, inconceivable depth of misery must the poor creature be sunk who will be the subject of this!

    Consider this, you+ who are here present and still remain in an unregenerate state. That God will execute the fierceness of his anger implies that he will inflict wrath without any pity. When God sees the unspeakable extremity of your+ case, and sees your+ torment to be so vastly out of proportion to your+ strength, and sees how your+ poor soul is crushed and sinks down, as it were, into an infinite gloom, he will have no compassion on you+. He will not hold back the execution of his wrath or lighten his hand in the least. There will be no moderation or mercy, nor will God then hold back his rough wind at all. He will have no regard for your+ welfare, nor be at all concerned lest you+ suffer too much in any other sense than only that you+ will not suffer beyond what strict justice requires. Nothing will be withheld because it is so hard for you+ to bear. “Therefore I will respond with wrath. I will not look on them with pity, nor will I spare them. Although they cry loudly in My ears, I will not listen to them” (Ezek 8:18). Now God stands ready to pity you+; this is a day of mercy; you+ may cry now with some encouragement of obtaining mercy. But once the day of mercy is past, your+ most mournful and sorrowful cries and shrieks will be in vain. You+ will be wholly lost and cast away by God, as far as any regard for your+ welfare is concerned. God will have no other use to put you+ to but to suffer misery; you+ will be kept in being for no other end. For you+ will be a vessel of wrath fitted for destruction, and there will be no other use for this vessel but to be filled full of wrath. God will be so far from pitying you+ when you+ cry to him that it is said he will only “laugh” and “mock” (Prov 1:25–26, etc.).

    How fearful are those words, the words of the great God (Isa 63:3): “I have trodden the winepress alone, and no one from the nations was with Me. I trampled them in My anger and trod them down in My fury; their blood was sprinkled on My garments, and all My clothes were stained.” It is perhaps impossible to conceive of words that carry in them greater displays of these three things: contempt, hatred, and fierceness of indignation. If you cry to God to pity you, he will be so far from pitying you in your doleful case, or showing you the least regard or favor, that instead he will only tread you underfoot. And though he will know that you cannot bear the weight of omnipotence treading on you, he will not regard that; he will crush you under his feet without mercy. He will crush out your blood and make it fly, and it will be sprinkled on his garments, so as to stain all his clothing. He will not only hate you, but he will hold you in the utmost contempt. No place will be thought fit for you but under his feet, to be trodden down like the mire of the streets.

  3. The misery you are exposed to is what God will inflict for this very end: to show what the wrath of Jehovah is. God has had it on his heart to show to angels and men both how excellent his love is and how terrible his wrath is. Earthly kings sometimes have a mind to show how terrible their wrath is by the extreme punishments they would carry out on those who provoke them. Nebuchadnezzar, that mighty and haughty monarch of the Chaldean empire, was determined to show his wrath when he was enraged with Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. Accordingly he gave orders that the burning fiery furnace should be heated seven times hotter than it was before; doubtless it was raised to the utmost degree of fierceness that human skill could raise it to. But the great God also intends to show his wrath, and to magnify his fearful majesty and mighty power in the extreme sufferings of his enemies. “What if God, intending to show His wrath and make His power known, bore with great patience the vessels of His wrath, prepared for destruction?” (Rom 9:22). And since this is his design, and what he has determined, namely to show how terrible the unrestrained wrath, the fury and fierceness of Jehovah, is, he will do it to full effect. Something will be accomplished and brought to pass that will be dreadful indeed. When the great and angry God has risen up and executed his fearful vengeance on the poor sinner, and the wretch is actually suffering the infinite weight and power of his indignation, then God will call on the whole universe to behold that fearful majesty and mighty power that is to be seen in it. “The peoples will be burned to ashes, like thorns cut down and set ablaze. You who are far off, hear what I have done; you who are near, acknowledge My might. The sinners in Zion are afraid; trembling grips the ungodly,” and so on (Isa 33:12–14). So it will be with you who are in an unconverted state, if you continue in it. The infinite might and majesty and terribleness of the omnipotent God will be magnified on you in the unspeakable strength of your torments. You will be tormented in the presence of the holy angels and in the presence of the Lamb. And when you are in this state of suffering, the glorious inhabitants of heaven will go forth and look on the fearful spectacle, so that they may see what the wrath and fierceness of the Almighty is; and when they have seen it, they will fall down and adore that great power and majesty. “From one New Moon to another and from one Sabbath to another, all mankind will come to worship before Me,” says the Lord. “As they go forth, they will see the corpses of the men who have rebelled against Me; for their worm will never die, their fire will never be quenched, and they will be a horror to all mankind” (Isa 66:23–24).

  4. It is everlasting wrath. It would be dreadful to suffer this fierceness and wrath of Almighty God for one moment, but you must suffer it to all eternity. There will be no end to this intense, horrible misery. When you look forward, you will see a long forever, a boundless duration before you, which will swallow up your thoughts and overwhelm your soul. You will absolutely despair of ever having any deliverance, any end, any easing, any rest at all. You will know for certain that you must wear out long ages, millions of millions of ages, in wrestling and struggling with this almighty, merciless vengeance. And then, when you have done so, when so many ages have actually been spent by you in this way, you will know that all of it is but a point compared with what remains. So your punishment will indeed be infinite. Oh, who can express what the state of a soul in such circumstances is! All that we can possibly say about it gives only a very feeble, faint picture of it; it is inexpressible and inconceivable. For “who knows the power of God’s anger?”

How dreadful is the state of those who are daily and hourly in danger of this great wrath and infinite misery! But this is the dismal case of every soul in this congregation that has not been born again, however moral and strict, sober and religious they may otherwise be. Oh, that you+ would consider it, whether you+ are young or old! There is reason to think that there are many in this congregation now hearing this sermon who will actually be the subjects of this very misery to all eternity. We do not know who they are, or in what seats they sit, or what thoughts they now have. It may be that they are now at ease, hearing all these things without much disturbance, and are now flattering themselves that they are not the ones, promising themselves that they will escape. If we knew that there was one person, and only one, in the whole congregation who was to be the subject of this misery, what a fearful thing it would be to think of! If we knew who it was, what a fearful sight it would be to see such a person! How might all the rest of the congregation lift up a mournful and bitter cry over him! But alas, instead of one, how many are likely to remember this sermon in hell! And it would be a wonder if some who are now present were not in hell in a very short time, even before this year is out. And it would be no wonder if some persons who now sit here, in some seats of this meeting house, in health, quiet and unconcerned, should be there before tomorrow morning. Those of you+ who continue to the end in a natural condition, even those who keep out of hell longest, will be there in a little while! Your+ damnation does not slumber; it will come swiftly, and in all probability very suddenly, on many of you+. You+ have reason to wonder that you+ are not already in hell. It is doubtless the case with some whom you+ have seen and known, who never deserved hell more than you+, and who once appeared as likely to be alive now as you+. Their case is past all hope; they are crying out in extreme misery and perfect despair. But here you+ are in the land of the living and in the house of God, and you+ have an opportunity to obtain salvation. What would those poor, damned, hopeless souls not give for one day’s opportunity such as you+ now enjoy!

And now you+ have an extraordinary opportunity. This is a day when Christ has thrown the door of mercy wide open. He stands in the door, calling and crying out with a loud voice to poor sinners. It is a day when many are flocking to him and pressing into the kingdom of God. Every day many are coming from the east and west, north and south. Many who were very recently in the same miserable condition you+ are in are now in a happy state. Their hearts are filled with love to him who loved them and washed them from their sins in his own blood, and they rejoice in hope of the glory of God. How dreadful it is to be left behind on such a day! To see so many others feasting while you+ are pining and perishing! To see so many rejoicing and singing for joy of heart while you+ have cause to mourn for sorrow of heart and to howl for anguish of spirit! How can you+ rest for one moment in such a condition? Are not your+ souls as precious as the souls of the people at Suffield1, who are flocking to Christ day after day?

Are there not many here who have lived long in the world and to this day are not born again, and so are aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and have done nothing, ever since they have lived, but store up wrath for the day of wrath? Oh, sirs, your+ case in particular is extremely dangerous. Your+ guilt and hardness of heart are extremely great. Do you+ not see how generally people of your+ years are passed over and left behind in the present remarkable and wonderful dispensation of God’s mercy? You+ need to consider yourselves+ and wake thoroughly out of sleep. You+ cannot bear the fierceness and wrath of the infinite God. And you+, young men and young women, will you+ neglect this precious season that you+ now enjoy, when so many others of your+ age are renouncing all youthful vanities and flocking to Christ? You+ especially have an extraordinary opportunity now; but if you+ neglect it, it will soon be with you+ as with those who spent all the precious days of youth in sin and have now come to such a dreadful pass in blindness and hardness. And you+, children, who are unconverted, do you+ not know that you+ are going down to hell to bear the dreadful wrath of that God who is now angry with you+ every day and every night? Will you+ be content to be the children of the devil, when so many other children in the land are converted and have become the holy and happy children of the King of kings?

And let everyone who is still outside of Christ and hanging over the pit of hell, whether old men and women, or middle-aged, or young people, or little children, now listen to the loud calls of God’s word and providence. This year of the Lord’s favor, a day of such great favor to some, will doubtless be a day of just as remarkable vengeance to others. Men’s hearts harden and their guilt increases rapidly at such a time as this, if they neglect their souls; and never was there so great a danger of such persons being given up to hardness of heart and blindness of mind. God seems now to be swiftly gathering in his elect in all parts of the land. Probably the greater part of the adults who will ever be saved will be brought in within a short time, and it will probably be as it was at the great outpouring of the Spirit on the Jews in the apostles’ days: the elect will obtain it, and the rest will be blinded. If this is the case with you, you will curse this day eternally, and will curse the day you were born to see such a season of the pouring out of God’s Spirit, and will wish that you had died and gone to hell before you had seen it. Now undoubtedly it is as it was in the days of John the Baptist: the axe is laid in an extraordinary way at the root of the trees, so that every tree that does not bring forth good fruit may be cut down and thrown into the fire.

Therefore let everyone who is outside of Christ wake up now and flee from the wrath to come. The wrath of almighty God is now, beyond doubt, hanging over a great part of this congregation. Let everyone flee out of Sodom: “Run for your lives! Do not look back, and do not stop anywhere on the plain! Flee to the mountains, or you will be swept away!” (Gen 19:17).
